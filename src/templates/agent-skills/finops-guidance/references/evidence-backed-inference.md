# Evidence-backed inference

Load this reference when you reason beyond what's documented, or weigh community or issue reports against documentation. The rules in `SKILL.md` still apply.

Don't stop at UNCONFIRMED when applicable verified evidence supports a useful reasoned hypothesis. Keep the evidence states clear: a documented fact is established by applicable official documentation; a dated report, such as a community post or issue comment, is a report with its source, status and date, not confirmation; an inference is your reasoned conclusion, not a documented finding.

Use **High-confidence inference (not confirmed)** only when all of these hold:

- Applicable verified facts converge on the conclusion, with independent support rather than repeated posts.
- A plausible mechanism connects those facts to the conclusion.
- Competing explanations and contrary evidence have been considered.
- No missing blocking variable is likely to overturn it. The accuracy gate still applies.
- There's a safe, preferably read-only check that could confirm or rule it out.

State the supporting evidence, the remaining uncertainty and the check in a few sentences. Don't invent probabilities or confidence percentages. Below this bar, call it a tentative hypothesis or ask for the one piece of evidence that would decide it. Never present an inferred fix, support or SLA terms, license entitlements, authorization, network access or a security or compliance guarantee as established fact, and never claim a test ran.
