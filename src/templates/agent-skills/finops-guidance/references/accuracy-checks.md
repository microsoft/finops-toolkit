# Accuracy checks for high-risk specifics

Load this reference before you answer about invoice reconciliation, identity, authorization and network access, dataset and report requirements, versions, current prices, known issues or support, and whenever a tool, export, report, query or agent fails or shows unexpected or empty results. The rules in `SKILL.md` still apply.

- Invoice reconciliation: don't equate an export's charges, usage period or cost field with the invoice total. Check the agreement, billing scope, invoice and usage periods, actual versus amortized or FOCUS semantics, adjustments, credits, tax, purchases and other included or excluded charges in the applicable documentation. State the boundary of the comparison. Claim an exact match only when the documentation and an actual reconciliation establish it; otherwise explain what remains to reconcile.
- Identity, authorization and network: role assignments and managed identities answer different questions from storage networking and firewall access. Check the configuring person's permissions separately from the service's run-time identity and scope, and check storage, firewall, networking, region and service prerequisites separately. Never infer network reachability from a role or from enabling a managed identity.
- Datasets and reports: a report's dataset requirements apply to that report and version. Don't present them as requirements for every export or workflow. Check the report's supported schema, granularity and prerequisites before recommending a dataset.
- Versions and alternatives: check the actual tool, report, schema and API versions instead of assuming the newest. Recommend a preview only when the evidence and the user's risk tolerance justify it. Don't describe an alternative as retired or unsupported without the applicable dated notice and a short exact quote.
- Current pricing: a current, applicable official source can support a price. Keep its plan, billing model, unit, currency, region, effective date and other conditions. If approved sources don't establish a rate, mark it UNCONFIRMED and use a clearly labeled, user-supplied assumption for any illustrative calculation. Leave out nonessential unverified prices rather than adding a rate card. Advice about when to commit or stay on pay-as-you-go is contextual judgment, not a product fact.
- Known issues and support: check the documented troubleshooting and error guidance and the toolkit changelog for the version in use. Distinguish a documented known issue or fix from similar reports in community posts or issue threads. Repeated reports aren't independent confirmation, and a closed issue doesn't prove its fix applies here. Prefer a low-risk, read-only check before disruptive remediation. Microsoft Support doesn't handle FinOps toolkit support requests, but the underlying managed products, such as Cost Management, are supported. Point to the toolkit's troubleshooting guide first, a Microsoft support request when the source of the problem is an underlying product, and a GitHub issue to report a toolkit problem.
- Troubleshooting triage: when a tool, export, report, query or agent fails or shows unexpected or empty results, sort the likely cause before recommending a fix. A product or deployment defect means something already in place is failing, misconfigured or contradicts its documentation, such as an error, a failed run or a documented bug in that version; name the failing component, the evidence and the documented fix or support path, checking Known issues and support first. A data sufficiency limit means the available data can't support the conclusion, such as a short window, a single billing period, a missing dataset or stale data; say what the data does show and what's missing. A customer-owned delegation means the next step needs a role, scope, delegated access, billing visibility setting or network access that the customer has to grant or configure; name it from the applicable documentation, applying Identity, authorization and network above, instead of treating it as a defect. If the result is correct as documented, such as a real cost change or a true zero, say so rather than forcing a category; cost changes follow Explaining a cost change in `references/cost-figures.md`. A problem can fall into more than one category. When the evidence doesn't separate them and the category changes the next step, ask the one question or suggest the one read-only check that best tells them apart, under the accuracy gate. Use the sort to choose that step, not as a visible template.
- Sparse data: don't turn thin data into a false defect or a false all-clear. With one billing period or a small sample, report point-in-time observations and mark trend, forecast, anomaly and period-over-period conclusions as limited or unavailable. Don't conclude that there's no anomaly, savings opportunity or risk unless the data's scope, period and freshness support it.
