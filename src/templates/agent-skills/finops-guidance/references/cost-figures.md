# Cost figures, cost changes and savings

Load this reference whenever you interpret or explain cost amounts, cost changes or savings, including figures from the user, a report or another agent. For AI costs, also load `references/ai-costs.md`. These rules set an investigation order and labeling rules, not a required response structure. The rules in `SKILL.md` still apply.

## Figures people share

- Before interpreting shared figures (pasted numbers, a screenshot, an export or another agent's answer, such as a FinOps hub agent set up in Copilot Studio with the toolkit's instructions), check what they show: source or tool, scope, period and whether it's complete, currency, cost measure, and included charges (usage, purchases, credits, adjustments, tax, Marketplace).
- Use what's stated or clearly visible, and state a material non-blocking gap as an assumption or open check. Ask one question under the accuracy gate only when a single missing item blocks the answer. When several items are missing because the data hasn't been pulled yet, make one data request as described below instead of a series of questions.
- Don't add, net or compare amounts in different currencies unless a sourced conversion is provided; report them separately.
- Keep shared figures out of public documentation queries, and don't treat a screenshot or export total as an invoice total without applying Invoice reconciliation in `references/accuracy-checks.md`.
- When the answer needs data the user doesn't have yet and no data tool is available, name the tool that can produce it, such as Cost Management cost analysis, exports, FinOps hubs or a FinOps hub agent, and say exactly what to bring back: scope type, period, cost measure, currency and the grouping that answers the question, aggregated, with tenant, billing account, subscription and resource identifiers replaced by placeholders unless they're needed.

## Explaining a cost change

Before calling a change real usage growth, work through the checks the available evidence can address. Say which material ones remain open, beside the conclusion they affect.

1. Comparable periods. Compare like for like: the same scope and cost measure, and complete periods, normalized to a daily average when their lengths differ, or the same elapsed days of each period when an in-period view is needed, labeled as estimated. Never compare a partial current period with a full prior one. Billing periods don't always follow calendar months, Cost Management treats open-month data as estimated, and recent charges can still change after a billing period ends. For current timing, check "Understand Cost Management data" (Cost and usage data updates and retention) instead of quoting a remembered number.
2. Cost measure. Confirm whether the figures are actual (billed) or amortized (effective) cost. In actual cost, a reservation or savings plan purchase appears as billed, either upfront in the purchase month or as a recurring monthly payment, and the usage it covers shows zero cost. Amortized cost spreads the purchase across the term and assigns it to the usage that consumed the benefit, unused commitment cost appears only in amortized cost, and amortized cost doesn't match the invoice. Check "View amortized benefit costs" for the applicable behavior. For Microsoft Online Services Agreement (MOSA) accounts, such as pay-as-you-go accounts that aren't under an MCA, Cost Management includes usage but not commitment or Marketplace purchases, so check those purchases on the invoice.
3. Purchases and other non-usage charges. Separate purchases, credits, adjustments and tax from usage before comparing usage trends. A purchase can be one-time or recurring, so check how often it's charged rather than assuming.
4. Marketplace. Report third-party Marketplace charges separately from Microsoft service charges. Most Marketplace purchases aren't amortized, so they appear in the purchase period in both cost measures; check "View amortized benefit costs" for the exception.
5. Rate versus usage. Distinguish a unit price or discount change from a quantity change, and a SKU, meter or resize change from growth in the same resource.
6. Commitment coverage. Lower reservation or savings plan coverage can raise on-demand cost. Don't attribute lower coverage to an expired commitment until the user confirms its end date or status from an authoritative source; a scope change, an exchange or new usage the commitment doesn't cover can also lower coverage.
7. Reconcile. The identified drivers should account for the change in the same cost measure and currency: the starting amount plus the identified drivers plus any unexplained remainder equals the ending amount. Report the remainder instead of forcing a cause.
8. Outlook. Label each material driver one-time, recurring or trending, meaning your assessment of whether it continues rather than the charge frequency in the data. When the evidence supports it, give a labeled estimate for the next period.

Never present a spike, change or cause as a finding unless the user reported it or the evidence shows it. Naming candidate drivers to check is fine when they're labeled as checks. Causal conclusions follow Evidence-backed inference in `references/evidence-backed-inference.md`.

## Naming measures and savings

- With every material cost figure, state the cost measure, currency and period, and match the cost measure to the question. Billed cost is the basis for invoicing and excludes amortization of upfront charges, so it suits invoice questions and when charges are billed, subject to Invoice reconciliation in `references/accuracy-checks.md`. Effective cost is amortized and includes the applicable portion of prepaid purchases, so it suits trends, showback and unit cost. Confirm the definitions in the "FinOps toolkit data dictionary" when precision matters.
- Every savings figure names its source, baseline and period, and whether it's realized or a projected estimate. For FinOps toolkit reports and hubs, use the definitions in "Understanding savings calculations in FinOps toolkit": negotiated discount savings is list cost minus contracted cost, commitment discount savings is contracted cost minus effective cost, and total savings is list cost minus effective cost. Missing list or contracted prices can show savings as zero, and effective cost above list or contracted cost shows as negative savings; say so when it affects the figure. For another tool's figure, such as an Azure Advisor estimate, use that tool's documented basis, and don't combine figures with different bases.
- Don't present a period-over-period spend reduction as discount savings. Say which comparison you mean.
